\ReportSection{Reference A: Executive-Level IR Concepts}
\ReportBody{This section intentionally avoids academic labels in the main narrative. These are seven discovery concepts a leadership team can understand and act on.}\par
\vspace{0.08in}
{\fontsize{7.65}{9.0}\selectfont
\begin{longtable}{@{}L{0.20\linewidth}L{0.37\linewidth}L{0.34\linewidth}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Concept} & \textbf{Executive explanation} & \textbf{Why it matters}\\
\midrule
\endhead
Normalization & Whether the site understands common typing variations such as missing apostrophes, plurals, shorthand, and gender wording. & Good normalization keeps high-intent shoppers from being punished for normal typing habits.\\
Tokenization & Whether the site handles multi-word phrases, slash notation, brand numerals, and product phrases as shopper intent rather than isolated words. & Token handling affects whether customers can search naturally instead of memorizing exact product names.\\
Synonyms & Whether the site connects customer language to retailer language: shell versus jacket, puffer versus insulated, pack versus backpack, and similar wording. & Synonym coverage is often where good search looks expert, because customers rarely use catalog-perfect terminology.\\
Ranking And Field Weighting & Whether important fields such as brand, product type, gender, activity, and model name appear to influence what rises first. & Strong ranking makes the first few results feel obvious, which is what owners notice when they test their own site.\\
Query Expansion & Whether broader needs such as trail running, hot-weather hiking, lake day, or gifts expand into useful products. & Expansion is where search moves from order-taking to selling: it helps a shopper who knows the use case but not the product name.\\
Document Enrichment & Whether searchable fields include useful product attributes such as UPF, waterproof, quick-dry, merino, polarized, packable, and breathable. & Enriched product data lets shoppers search by benefits, materials, and features instead of exact titles.\\
Spanish/Bilingual Discovery & Whether Spanish or mixed Spanish-English searches can still guide local and visiting shoppers to the right English-language products. & This is a market-access question, not a demographic assumption: bilingual discovery reduces friction when customers search in Spanish, English, or both.\\
\bottomrule
\end{longtable}
}
