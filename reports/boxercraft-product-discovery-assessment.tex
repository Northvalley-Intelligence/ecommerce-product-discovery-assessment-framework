\item \Bad{Priority team discovery is not protected.} UCLA/Bruins terms were mostly recoverable at 7/8 search and 6/8 autocomplete, but \Code{UCLA quarter zip} dropped in autocomplete and \Code{Bruins apparel} returned 0 search results.
\item \Warn{Competitive pressure remains in licensed-team visibility.} Colosseum exposed more UCLA/Bruins public catalog matches than Boxercraft in the tested direct-site feeds, 20 versus 6.
\end{itemize}

\ReportPageHeader{Detailed Findings}
\ReportFooter
\ReportSection{Detailed Findings}
\begin{minipage}[t]{0.48\linewidth}
\FindingBlock{Finding 01 / High}{Search and autocomplete normalization are materially uneven}{\textbf{Observation:} The 36-query Normalization Matrix shows quarter-zip variants at 2/6 relevant in search and 3/6 in autocomplete, women's fleece variants at 3/5 on both surfaces, and color-plus-product variants at 4/5 in search but 0/5 in autocomplete.\\[0.04in]\textbf{Customer impact:} Customers using common spacing, abbreviation, gender, or color phrasing can receive a false dead-end signal.\\[0.04in]\textbf{Recommendation:} Create a shared normalization map for spacing, punctuation, abbreviations, gender synonyms, and color-plus-product phrasing, then apply it consistently to search and autocomplete.}
\vspace{0.12in}
\FindingBlock{Finding 02 / High}{Priority fan-shop and team discovery is not protected}{\textbf{Observation:} UCLA/Bruins terms were mostly recoverable, but \Code{UCLA quarter zip} dropped in autocomplete and \Code{Bruins apparel} returned 0 search results. Team aliases also remained uneven: \Code{team apparel}, \Code{Bulldogs}, and \Code{Georgia Bulldogs} returned 0 search results.\\[0.04in]\textbf{Customer impact:} A fan-shop shopper can find some UCLA paths but still hit dead ends for likely team, mascot, or product-specific language.\\[0.04in]\textbf{Recommendation:} Treat UCLA/Bruins and other priority team/mascot/university aliases as protected relevance sets with search, autocomplete, and zero-result recovery checks.}
\end{minipage}
\hfill
\begin{minipage}[t]{0.46\linewidth}
\FindingBlock{Finding 03 / High}{Browse-node, search, and autocomplete parity is not reliable}{\textbf{Observation:} GEN-007 evaluated 93 non-empty public browse nodes and found exact search/browse product-set parity on only 5/60 category or fan-shop nodes. \Code{blankets} returned 2 browse products, while search and autocomplete reported 3 products; the missing browse product was \Code{Lambert Soccer Everest Blanket}.\\[0.04in]\textbf{Customer impact:} Customers can see different product availability depending on whether they browse, search, or type into autocomplete.\\[0.04in]\textbf{Recommendation:} Make all-node browse parity a required gate: each priority collection should have matching collection membership, search indexing, autocomplete coverage, and fallback routing before parity is reported.}
\vspace{0.12in}
\FindingBlock{Finding 04 / Medium}{Product-page recommendations are weakly related}{\textbf{Observation:} \Code{You May Also Like} rendered on a quarter-zip product page, but the captured recommendation repeated the same product.\\[0.04in]\textbf{Customer impact:} Shoppers receive little help discovering alternatives or complements.\\[0.04in]\textbf{Recommendation:} Tune recommendations toward distinct similar products, complements, or collection alternatives.}
\end{minipage}
